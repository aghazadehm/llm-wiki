# Impact Mapping

Impact Mapping is a strategic planning technique for connecting business goals to deliverables through a map of actors and desired impacts. Created by Gojko Adzic, it answers "Why are we doing this?" before asking "What should we build?", preventing scope creep and ensuring work traces to measurable outcomes.

## Related Concepts

- [[User Story Mapping]] — takes high-priority deliverables from impact maps and organizes them along the user journey for release planning.
- [[Outcome Over Output]] — the core principle impact mapping enforces; focus on impacts (behavior change) not just deliverables.
- [[Feature Injection]] — shares the goal of ensuring features deliver business value; impact mapping provides the structure to do this systematically.
- [[Outside-In Development]] — impact mapping starts from business goals and works outward through actors/impacts to deliverables.

## My Understanding

Impact mapping works as a mind map with four levels: Goal (center), Actors (who can help/hinder), Impacts (how behavior must change), and Deliverables (what to build/do). The power is traceability — every deliverable must answer "Why are we building this?" by linking back through impacts to actors and ultimately to a measurable business goal. 

What makes it effective is that it exposes assumptions. By visualizing the chain of reasoning, stakeholders can scrutinize whether the proposed deliverables actually create the desired impacts on the right actors. It naturally generates prioritization (which impacts on which actors are most important?) and helps discard deliverables with no clear path to the goal. 

Impact mapping is strategic, not tactical — it decides *what's worth doing*, not *how to build it*. It's most valuable at initiative/quarter level planning when there's risk of building the wrong thing.
